**SwiftForms** collects submissions from the forms you already have on your
website and delivers them to a shared inbox in your SwiftAgent dashboard. You
keep your existing markup, your existing styling, and your existing backend. You
add one script tag, and every `<form>` on the page starts reporting what visitors
send you.

It is a separate script from the [chat widget](/introduction). The two products
share a dashboard and a company account, but they load independently, so you can
run either one on its own or both together.

## The two kinds of form

SwiftAgent distinguishes between forms that live on your own site and forms that
SwiftAgent hosts for you.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Website forms" icon="globe">
    You already have a contact form, a signup form, or a quote request on your
    site. SwiftForms attaches to it and mirrors each submission into your inbox
    without changing how the form behaves.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Online forms" icon="file-lines">
    You do not have a form yet, or you want one that lives outside your site.
    SwiftAgent gives you a hosted form with its own URL that you can link or
    share directly.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The difference matters in a few places, so it is worth knowing which one you
created:

| | Website form | Online form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the form lives | Your site | Hosted by SwiftAgent |
| What you configure | Website link, alert email | Form title, form image |
| How visitors reach it | Your existing page | A shareable `form_url` |
| Installation | Paste the snippet on your site | No installation needed |
| Reply from the inbox | Not available | Available |

## What makes it safe to add

The capture script was written so that adding it to a production site is a low
risk change.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="It never blocks a submission" icon="shield-check">
    The script does not call `preventDefault()`. Your form submits exactly as it
    did before, whether that is a native POST or a React handler.
  </Card>
  <Card title="It fails quietly" icon="volume-off">
    If SwiftAgent is unreachable, the network error is swallowed. Nothing is
    logged to the visitor's console and nothing breaks on the page.
  </Card>
  <Card title="It skips secrets" icon="key">
    Passwords, file inputs, and CSRF tokens are never collected. See
    [field capture](/forms/field-capture) for the full list.
  </Card>
  <Card title="It has no dependencies" icon="feather">
    Roughly 12 KB of plain ES5. No framework, no bundler, no build step.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How a submission travels

<Steps>
  <Step title="A visitor submits your form">
    The visitor fills in your form and clicks your submit button. Your own
    handling runs untouched.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The script reads the field values">
    SwiftForms walks the form's elements, collects the submittable values, and
    skips anything sensitive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A copy is sent to SwiftAgent">
    A `fetch` with `keepalive: true` posts the values, the page URL, a form name,
    and a visitor ID. `keepalive` lets the request finish even if the page
    navigates away immediately after submitting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It lands in your inbox">
    The submission appears under **Ticketing → Forms**, grouped by website, then
    page, then form. If you set an alert email, SwiftAgent emails you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Where forms live in the dashboard

Forms are a tab inside **Ticketing**, alongside tickets and mail. Open
[swiftagents.org](https://swiftagents.org), go to **Ticketing**, and select the
**Forms** tab.

<Note>
  Forms and the chat widget are not connected. The AI agent does not read form
  submissions and cannot show a form inside a chat conversation. If you want a
  visitor's message to reach your team through the agent, use
  [support tickets](/features/chat) instead.
</Note>
